# Lessons from iteration

- “Real potato + handmade doll + studio photography” drifted into polished collectible photography, realistic eyelids and thick fluffy bedding. Avoid those phrases when the target is a coarse sticker.
- Original visual references constrain the face and texture better than text alone. Give each input a role: character, prop, material or pose; explicitly discard scene-specific features.
- More pixelation is not the same as weaker physical realism. Hard low-poly facets made one blanket look like origami, while a clean laptop still looked pasted in.
- Props need the same coarse fidelity as the potato. Lower the geometry and shading of the laptop, racket, cup, mixer or blanket together.
- When a blanket feels too real, flatten its geometry, remove deep folds and contact occlusion, and let the check pattern carry recognition.
- For a readable visual joke, specify cause-and-effect silhouettes and object relationships. “A potato near fries” is weaker than “the intact upper potato is above the cutter and its connected lower body becomes straight sticks below the grid.”
- Keep user-approved examples as references, but do not inherit their old expression, props, clothing or background into a new scene.
- Verify actual PNG dimensions. A compatible service previously returned 1254×1254 despite a 1024×1024 request, so the workflow preserves the original and makes a verified square delivery.
